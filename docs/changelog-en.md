# Laprdus — Version History

This file lists what changed for users between released versions of Laprdus, on every platform. Changes made during the development of a version (alpha, beta and release candidate builds) are not listed separately: each entry describes the released version as a whole.

## Version 2.0

### All platforms

- Three new voices synthesized entirely by rule, with no recordings: Zvonko, Stojan and Mirsad. They stay clear and intelligible at very high speech rates, because rate and pitch are not obtained by processing recorded sound afterwards.
- Bosnian is now a supported language.
- Slovenian is now a supported language, spoken by every voice. Its pronunciation follows the Slovenski pravopis: the open and close *e* and *o* (*mésto*, *člôvek*), the schwa (*veter*, *pes*, *misel*), the *l* and *v* that become [u̯] (*bil*, *delal*, *stol*, *nov*, *vlak*), *lj* and *nj* as two sounds before a vowel, the devoicing at the end of a word (*grad*) and the voicing assimilation (*k bratu*, *s časom*). Every word is stressed on the right syllable with the right length from a built-in dictionary of 25,000 words with all their forms, taken from the Sloleks lexicon of the University of Ljubljana, and by rules for the rest. Numbers are read the Slovenian way (*ena, dve, tri; enaindvajset; dva tisoč; dva milijona, trije milijoni, pet milijonov; dve milijardi*), decimals as *tri cele štirinajst*, a period between groups of three digits as the thousands separator (*132.457*), dates in the genitive (*7. 10. 2026* is *sedmega desetega dva tisoč šestindvajset*), and letters, digits, punctuation and symbols are named in Slovenian when spelling (*be, ce, če; ena, dve; pika, vejica, afna*). The accent dictionary takes Slovenian entries in the notation of the Slovenian dictionaries (*člôvek*, *govoríti*, *d'é:la\**). There is no Slovenian voice of its own: Zvonko, Stojan, Mirsad, Josip and the other voices speak Slovenian with their own colour, and no voice has Slovenian as its native language.
- Every voice speaks every language. The voice and the language are chosen separately, as in eSpeak or Eloquence: Josip or Zvonko can speak Serbian, Vlado or Stojan Croatian, any voice Bosnian or Slovenian. The language chooses the pronunciation rules, the accent dictionary, the number words and the letter names; the voice chooses only the sound. How the language is chosen depends on the platform: the Language setting of the NVDA add-on (NVDA's automatic language switching also switches among Croatian, Serbian, Bosnian and Slovenian), one of the four SAPI5 voices Laprdus Croatian, Laprdus Serbian, Laprdus Bosnian and Laprdus Slovenian, the language of the system voice on Android and on Apple devices (every voice is offered under all four languages), the Language setting of the Android and Apple apps for their own Speak button, the `-L` option of the command-line tool, and the language of a Speech Dispatcher request. Without any choice, a voice speaks its native language: Croatian for Josip, Detence, Baba and Zvonko, Serbian for Vlado, Đedo and Stojan, Bosnian for Mirsad.
- Zvonko is the default voice on every platform and for every language. A voice you have chosen yourself is never replaced. Josip, Vlado, Detence, Baba and Đedo remain available.
- Some voices have new names. The grandfather voice is Đedo (it was shown as Djed or Djedo), the child voice Detence and the grandmother Baba everywhere. The singing voices no longer carry the name of the speaking voice in front: Zvonko Orguljaš is now Orguljaš - Vila Velebita, Klapa Zvonko is Dalmacija Klapa, Stojan Trubač is Trubač - Kreće se lađa francuska, Stojan Harmonikaš is Harmonikaš - Kreće se lađa francuska, Mirsad Sevdalija is Sevdalija - Kad ja pođoh na Bembašu, Mirsad Sazlija is Sazlija - Kad ja pođoh na Bembašu, Zvonko Pjevač is Lika Velebit, Stojan Pevač is Solunac, Mirsad Solist is Sarajevo Bembaša and Zvonko Bećarac is Bećarac. Saved settings and scripts that use the old names of the voices (`djed`, `klapa`, `pjevac`, `pevac`, `solist`) keep working.
- Word stress: the new voices place the accent of each word using a built-in dictionary of several thousand words and names, rules for common endings and word classes (loans and derived nouns with a long last syllable such as *telefóna, programéra, prodaváča, zaposleníka*; prefixed verbs in all their forms, *poglèdala, napràvio*; verbs in *-ovati* and *-laziti*, nouns in *-ica*, *-anin* and *-teljstvo*, adjectives in *-antan*, *-ljiv* and *-ijski*, *interesàntno, nevìdljiv, simulácijski*; compounds of age and time such as *petogòdišnjī, šestòmjesečnī, malòljetan*), tables of towns, villages and regions and of given names and surnames of Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Slovenia with all their case forms as the dictionaries give them (*Knȋn, iz Knína, u Púli, Slàvōnija; Ánte, Dárko, Mȃrko, Lȗka, Tàmara, Júrić, Stárčević, Rȁdomīr*; used for words written with a capital letter, so a village or a name never changes the common word it is spelled like), and the accent marks you can write in the pronunciation dictionary (for example `telèfon`). The old voices stressed nothing.
- English words and names are stressed where English stresses them, while still being read with the letters' own sounds: *Croàtian*, *Ábleton*, *compùter*, *notificàtion*. Their stressed syllable is simply higher, without the rising tone of our own words, which made the next syllable sound stressed (ChatGPT sounded like "chatg-PT"). A word written with capitals inside it is read as its parts: *ElevenLabs* as *Eleven Labs*, *ChatGPT* as *Chat GPT*, while *TalkBack*, *TikTok*, *iPhone* and *BiH* stay whole. Croatian, Serbian and Bosnian words are not affected.
- Sentence melody in the manner of a Croatian radio announcer: a sentence starts high, keeps the voice on a level with a movement on each stressed word, and falls on its last word at a full stop. A comma gets a small fall and a slight rise, and the next part of the sentence starts again a little lower than the sentence did. A question rises at its end (also after a question word, such as "Kako si ti?"), and an exclamation has a wider range.
- Josip, Vlado and the voices derived from them have a new speech engine. They now have the same word stress and sentence melody as the new voices, use the accent dictionary and the accent marks of the pronunciation dictionary, speak at a natural tempo (Vlado's drawn-out vowels are gone) and keep the bursts of p, t, k, b, d and g audible at high rates. The clicks and crackles between sounds are gone.
- Changing the speech rate of Josip and Vlado no longer changes their pitch, and changing the pitch no longer makes the voice rough or muffled. Detence, Baba and Đedo now sound like a child, a grandmother and a grandfather instead of a sped-up or slowed-down recording.
- Ten singing voices: Orguljaš - Vila Velebita, Dalmacija Klapa, Lika Velebit and Bećarac (Zvonko's voice, Croatian songs), Trubač - Kreće se lađa francuska, Harmonikaš - Kreće se lađa francuska and Solunac (Stojan's voice, a Serbian song), Sevdalija - Kad ja pođoh na Bembašu, Sazlija - Kad ja pođoh na Bembašu and Sarajevo Bembaša (Mirsad's voice, a Bosnian song). They sing any text to a folk song, one syllable per note, in whichever of the three languages is chosen; the speech rate sets the tempo, the pitch transposes the song and the inflection level sets the depth of the vibrato.
- Two new settings for every voice: Inflection level (0% is a monotone, 50% is the natural melody, 100% doubles every movement) and Acceleration (a multiplier of the speech rate, from 0.5 to 3, so the rate slider can reach a higher or lower top speed; Josip, Vlado and the voices derived from them stop at four times the normal rate). They sit with the other voice settings on every platform.
- The speech rate and pitch set in Laprdus now work together with those of your screen reader or app. They are the voice's normal rate and pitch, and the rate and pitch that TalkBack, VoiceOver, NVDA or another program asks for speed them up, slow them down, raise or lower them from there: while the screen reader speaks at its normal rate, Laprdus speaks at exactly the rate you set. Before, they were used only with the matching Force switch on, so moving the rate slider changed nothing a screen reader said. The Force switches now make Laprdus ignore the rate or pitch the program asks for.
- Spelling by letter sounds: a new switch in the advanced settings lets you choose whether spelled and typed letters are read by their names (*be, ce, de*) or by the sounds they stand for (*b* as "bə", *f* as "fə", *h* as "hə"), the way a child learns to read and the way eSpeak sounds the letters out. Only spelling and typing are affected; words are spoken as before. Digits, punctuation and symbols keep their names, and so do the foreign letters *q, w, x, y* (*ku, duplo ve, iks, ipsilon*), which have no sound of their own in the language. A capital letter that the screen reader announces with a word before it ("veliko N" in NVDA, "veliko slovo N" in TalkBack, "veliko početno slovo N" in VoiceOver, "cap N" in English) is spelled like any other letter: by its sound when you chose letter sounds, and at the spelling speed.
- Spelling speed: the setting now controls pauses after spelled and typed characters. Every character uses the normal speech rate. Lower values add longer pauses; 100% adds no extra pause and the default is 50%. The configured spelling pause is added on top, including after the last character.
- Letter names follow the chosen language, with every voice. In Croatian and Bosnian the letters have the names of the Croatian orthography (*a, be, ce, če, će, de, dže, đe, e, ef, ge, ha, i, je, ka, el, elj, em, en, enj, o, pe, er, es, eš, te, u, ve, ze, že*); in Serbian they follow the Board for the Standardization of the Serbian Language (*lje, nje, ša*), for Cyrillic and Latin letters alike. "J" is now *je*, not *jot*. The spelling dictionary still names digits, punctuation and symbols; an entry of yours for a letter still wins, except that an entry which is merely a letter's name gives way when letters are spelled by their sounds.
- The new voices accept a wider range of speech rate and pitch: a quarter to four times the normal value.
- The voice settings are in the same order on every platform: speech rate and acceleration, then pitch and inflection, then volume. The intonation on/off switch of version 1.0 is gone; the inflection level replaces it, and 0% is a monotone.
- Accent dictionary: a new user dictionary, `accents.json`, lets you correct the stress of a word for all its forms at once, or of a whole verb with all its persons and tenses, for every voice. An entry can be limited to one language (Croatian, Serbian, Bosnian or Slovenian), whichever voice speaks it; a Slovenian entry is written in the notation of the Slovenian dictionaries (*člôvek*, *mésto*, *otròk*). It is stored next to the other user dictionaries and loaded with them, and it is edited like them: in the Laprdus Configurator on Windows (dictionary type Accent dictionary), in the Android app and in the app for iPhone, iPad and Mac, with fields for the entry, its kind (word or verb), its language and a comment. The editor checks every entry with the same rules Laprdus reads it by and refuses a malformed one with the reason. See section 5.11 of the user guide.
- Numbers are read in the chosen language, by every voice, Josip and Vlado included: *tisuća* and *milijun* in Croatian, *hiljada*, *milion* and *dvesta* in Serbian, *hiljada*, *milion* and *dvjesto* in Bosnian. One and two now agree with the number word (*dvije tisuće*, *dvadeset jedna tisuća*, *dvije milijarde*).
- For developers: the C API has `laprdus_set_language()` and `laprdus_get_language()` (any voice in any language; the language stays across voice changes), `laprdus_get_language_count()` and `laprdus_get_language_info()` (code, short code, Windows LCID, English and native name of each language) and `laprdus_get_default_voice()`; the `language_code` of a voice is now its native language.
- A colon or exclamation mark inside a word is always read by name, whatever punctuation level the screen reader uses, as eSpeak does. A period inside a word is read by name only before a file extension or a domain label ("datoteka.txt" is *datoteka točka te iks te*, "pjesma.mp3", "www.index.hr") and as a decimal point; in Serbian and Bosnian it is *tačka*. Anywhere else a period glued to the next word ends the sentence silently, the way screen readers glue the parts of a label: "Preslušano.Nestajuća poruka" is read as two sentences, without *točka*. A period after a single letter is the dot of an abbreviation: it is silent and the letters are read by name, "s.a.r.s." is *es a er es*, "U.S.A." *u es a*. A period between digits is read by name and every group of digits as a whole number: "3.14" is *tri točka četrnaest*, "1.317" *jedan točka tristo sedamnaest*, a version number or IP address is read group by group; "3,14" is *tri zarez četrnaest*. A comma between the single digits of a phone number, as screen readers send it ("+ 1 2 3,5 6,7 8 9,8 7 6"), is a pause between the groups, not *zarez*. A number with a period before a lowercase word is read as a plain number, with the period silent: "7. listopada 2026." is *sedam listopada dvije tisuće dvadeset šest*. A number glued to letters is set off from them ("u20:03" is *u dvadeset nula tri*, "5kg" *pet ka ge*), and the period that joins a word to such letters, as in a screen reader's label "7.listopada.u20:03", is silent: *sedam listopada u dvadeset nula tri*. Clock times and dates are read without the separators: "12:30" is *dvanaest trideset*, "7.10.2026." and "7. 10. 2026." *sedam deset dvije tisuće dvadeset šest*. The colon of a web address ("https://") is not read. Abbreviations are read as written, not expanded: "dr." is *de er*, "km" *ka em*. Before, every period, comma and colon split the text into clauses, so "datoteka.txt" was spoken as two sentences. In Slovenian the period inside a word is *pika*, "3,14" is *tri cele štirinajst*, "1.317" and "132.457" are whole numbers, and "7. 10. 2026" is *sedmega desetega dva tisoč šestindvajset*.
- Fixed: entries added to the user spelling and emoji dictionaries had no effect; they now change how characters and emoji are read.
- Fixed: on the command line and under Speech Dispatcher, Detence, Baba and Đedo sounded exactly like Josip and Vlado.
- Fixed: the newline pause setting had no effect, and a line break was read as a space, so the lines of a post or a list ran together into one sentence. A line break now ends a phrase with that pause, and the next line starts a sentence of its own: in a post whose line "Javno" is followed by the line "S ponosom predstavljam", *S* is the preposition, not the letter *es*.
- Volume boost: a new switch under the volume, off by default, makes the speech louder than the volume slider at 100% allows, for noisy places (traffic, the street) and the phone's earpiece with the speakerphone off: to hear whom you are calling while the call is being set up, or to find the speakerphone button during a call. The speech goes through a strong gain, a peak limiter and a soft clipper that rounds off the strongest peaks, as Google's "Amplify speech volume" does: the quiet parts come up by about 11 dB and the loud ones by about 8 dB, and the sound never breaks up, although the loudest vowels are slightly compressed. Your volume setting, and the screen reader's or the app's, still scale the boosted speech. Every voice has it.

### Windows (SAPI5 and NVDA)

- The new voices, the singing voices, the Inflection level and Acceleration settings and the accent dictionary are available in SAPI5 applications, in the Laprdus Configurator and in the NVDA add-on. The NVDA add-on has a Language setting (Croatian, Serbian, Bosnian or Slovenian, set from NVDA's language at first) next to the voice list, which shows every voice for every language; Zvonko is its default voice; it reloads the accent dictionary when the file changes.
- SAPI5 now registers four voices, Laprdus Croatian, Laprdus Serbian, Laprdus Bosnian and Laprdus Slovenian, instead of one per Laprdus voice. The SAPI5 voice chooses the language; all four speak with the voice chosen in the Laprdus Configurator (Zvonko until you change it; the voice is `voice.default` in `settings.json`, and there is no language key, because the program that speaks chooses the language). The installer removes the voices registered by earlier versions on install, upgrade and repair.
- The Laprdus Configurator has the Spelling speed slider in its pause settings and the Spelling choice (letter names or letter sounds) among the options; both apply to SAPI5 and to the NVDA add-on. The command-line tool has the new options `-s` (spell the text), `-S` (spelling speed) and `-m` (spelling mode).
- Fixed: when a program asked SAPI5 to spell only part of a text, Laprdus spelled all of it, and a requested silence was spoken as a space.
- NVDA: a pause that NVDA asks for is now real silence of the requested length; it was spoken as a space. NVDA's option "Delayed descriptions for characters on cursor movement" therefore works as NVDA intends: when you move to a letter, its description (for example "Alfa" for A) follows after exactly the second NVDA waits, and the Laprdus spelling speed adds no pause of its own before it. Spelling a word and typing keep the spelling speed's cadence.
- Settings saved by version 1.0 keep their meaning: an unforced speech rate or pitch, which version 1.0 ignored, stays normal instead of changing the rate the screen reader asks for, and the intonation switch of version 1.0, which controlled a different effect, is not carried over: the Inflection level starts at its default of 50%. Under NVDA, NVDA's Inflection slider sets the intonation, and NVDA's rate boost, not the Acceleration setting, extends the rate range.

### Linux

- The new voices, the singing voices and the accent dictionary are available in the command-line tool and in Speech Dispatcher. The command-line tool has the new options `-I` (inflection, 0-100), `-a` (acceleration), `-s` (spell the text), `-S` (spelling speed) and `-m` (spelling mode); under Speech Dispatcher the pitch range setting controls the inflection level, and the spelling speed and mode come from `settings.json`.
- Speech Dispatcher now offers Laprdus for Bosnian and Slovenian as well as Croatian and Serbian; the packages add the mapping on upgrade too. Every voice is listed for every language, a language request (`spd-say -l sr`) changes the language and keeps the voice, and the symbolic voices are the same in all four languages: MALE1 is Zvonko, MALE2 Josip, MALE3 Đedo, FEMALE1 Baba, CHILD_MALE Detence. The command-line tool has the `-L` option (`hr`, `sr`, `bs`, `sl`) and `-l` lists every voice with its native language.

### Android

- The new voices and the singing voices are available as system voices, with the Inflection level and Acceleration settings in the app. The Spelling speed slider is in the reading pauses and the Spelling switch (letter sounds) in the advanced settings.
- Laprdus now offers Bosnian and Slovenian to the system, and every voice under all four languages (72 system voices). The language an app or TalkBack asks for sets the language, the voice stays the one chosen in the app, and Zvonko is the default. When the phone's language is one Laprdus speaks, that language is spoken everywhere, by the Speak button in the app too, and the app's Language setting shows it; "Force language" makes the language chosen in that setting speak everywhere instead, whatever language the phone and the apps ask for. A phone in another language speaks the language of the setting.
- Fixed: the spelling and emoji dictionaries edited in the app were not used by the speech service; all three user dictionaries now take effect as soon as a voice is loaded. The accent dictionary is read from the same folder.
- Fixed: the app and the speech service shared one engine, so the Speak button spoke at the rate, pitch and volume of TalkBack's last request, and choosing a voice in the app left TalkBack without your dictionary entries until a voice was loaded again. Each now has an engine of its own: the Speak button speaks with your settings and your dictionaries, and TalkBack switches to the voice you choose with your dictionaries in place.
- A dictionary entry you add, change or delete in the app, and switching the user dictionaries on or off, now take effect in TalkBack at once. Before, they waited until a voice was loaded again.

### iPhone, iPad and Mac

- The new voices and the singing voices appear among the system voices, with the Inflection level and Acceleration settings in the app. The Spelling speed slider is in the reading pauses and the Spelling choice (letter names or letter sounds) in the advanced settings. The accent dictionary is read from the app's dictionary folder.
- Every voice is listed under Croatian, Serbian, Bosnian and Slovenian in Spoken Content and in VoiceOver's voice settings; the entry you pick chooses both the voice and the language. The app has a Language picker for its own Speak button, and Zvonko is the default voice.
- Fixed: when VoiceOver read an item that contains a single character to be spelled out, such as a button with a badge count, the whole item was spelled letter by letter, and the name and the type of a control ran together into one phrase. Each part is now read as VoiceOver asks: text as text, the character spelled, pauses as pauses.
- Fixed: after a character that VoiceOver spells inside an item, such as the badge count of a Notifications tab, there was a long pause before the rest of the item. The spelling pause and the pause between the parts of an item were both added; now only the pause between the parts is heard, and the parts of an item follow each other evenly.
- VoiceOver's pitch change for capital letters is now heard, a request that VoiceOver cancels is no longer spoken, and the spelling and emoji dictionaries are loaded together with the pronunciation dictionary.
- Fixed: VoiceOver's volume was ignored unless "Force Laprdus speech volume" was on, so the voices always spoke at full volume. With the force switch off, the volume VoiceOver asks for is now followed.
