# Laprdus — Version History

This file lists what changed for users between released versions of Laprdus, on every platform. Changes made during the development of a version (alpha, beta and release candidate builds) are not listed separately: each entry describes the released version as a whole.

## Version 2.0

### All platforms

- Three new voices synthesized entirely by rule, with no recordings: Zvonko (Croatian), Stojan (Serbian) and Mirsad (Bosnian). They stay clear and intelligible at very high speech rates, because rate and pitch are not obtained by processing recorded sound afterwards.
- Bosnian is now a supported language, with Mirsad as its voice.
- The new voices are the default voice of their language on every platform. A voice you have chosen yourself is never replaced. Josip, Vlado, Detence, Baba and Đedo remain available.
- Word stress: the new voices place the accent of each word using a built-in dictionary of several thousand words and names, rules for common endings and word classes (loans and derived nouns with a long last syllable such as *telefóna, programéra, prodaváča, zaposleníka*; prefixed verbs in all their forms, *poglèdala, napràvio*; verbs in *-ovati* and *-laziti*, nouns in *-ica*, *-anin* and *-teljstvo*, adjectives in *-antan*, *-ljiv* and *-ijski*, *interesàntno, nevìdljiv, simulácijski*; compounds of age and time such as *petogòdišnjī, šestòmjesečnī, malòljetan*), tables of towns, villages and regions and of given names and surnames of Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Slovenia with all their case forms as the dictionaries give them (*Knȋn, iz Knína, u Púli, Slàvōnija; Ánte, Dárko, Mȃrko, Lȗka, Tàmara, Júrić, Stárčević, Rȁdomīr*; used for words written with a capital letter, so a village or a name never changes the common word it is spelled like), and the accent marks you can write in the pronunciation dictionary (for example `telèfon`). The old voices stressed nothing.
- English words and names are stressed where English stresses them, while still being read with the letters' own sounds: *Croàtian*, *Ábleton*, *compùter*, *notificàtion*. Their stressed syllable is simply higher, without the rising tone of our own words, which made the next syllable sound stressed (ChatGPT sounded like "chatg-PT"). A word written with capitals inside it is read as its parts: *ElevenLabs* as *Eleven Labs*, *ChatGPT* as *Chat GPT*, while *TalkBack*, *TikTok*, *iPhone* and *BiH* stay whole. Croatian, Serbian and Bosnian words are not affected.
- Sentence melody in the manner of a Croatian radio announcer: a sentence starts high, keeps the voice on a level with a movement on each stressed word, and falls on its last word at a full stop. A comma gets a small fall and a slight rise, and the next part of the sentence starts again a little lower than the sentence did. A question rises at its end (also after a question word, such as "Kako si ti?"), and an exclamation has a wider range.
- Josip, Vlado and the voices derived from them have a new speech engine. They now have the same word stress and sentence melody as the new voices, use the accent dictionary and the accent marks of the pronunciation dictionary, speak at a natural tempo (Vlado's drawn-out vowels are gone) and keep the bursts of p, t, k, b, d and g audible at high rates. The clicks and crackles between sounds are gone.
- Changing the speech rate of Josip and Vlado no longer changes their pitch, and changing the pitch no longer makes the voice rough or muffled. Detence, Baba and Đedo now sound like a child, a grandmother and a grandfather instead of a sped-up or slowed-down recording.
- Ten singing voices: Zvonko Orguljaš, Klapa Zvonko, Zvonko Pjevač and Zvonko Bećarac (Croatian), Stojan Trubač, Stojan Harmonikaš and Stojan Pevač (Serbian), Mirsad Sevdalija, Mirsad Sazlija and Mirsad Solist (Bosnian). They sing any text to a folk song of their country, one syllable per note; the speech rate sets the tempo, the pitch transposes the song and the inflection level sets the depth of the vibrato.
- Two new settings for every voice: Inflection level (0% is a monotone, 50% is the natural melody, 100% doubles every movement) and Acceleration (a multiplier of the speech rate, from 0.5 to 3, so the rate slider can reach a higher or lower top speed; Josip, Vlado and the voices derived from them stop at four times the normal rate). They sit with the other voice settings on every platform.
- The speech rate and pitch set in Laprdus now work together with those of your screen reader or app. They are the voice's normal rate and pitch, and the rate and pitch that TalkBack, VoiceOver, NVDA or another program asks for speed them up, slow them down, raise or lower them from there: while the screen reader speaks at its normal rate, Laprdus speaks at exactly the rate you set. Before, they were used only with the matching Force switch on, so moving the rate slider changed nothing a screen reader said. The Force switches now make Laprdus ignore the rate or pitch the program asks for.
- Spelling by letter sounds: a new switch in the advanced settings lets you choose whether spelled and typed letters are read by their names (*be, ce, de*) or by the sounds they stand for (*b* as "bə", *f* as "fə", *h* as "hə"), the way a child learns to read and the way eSpeak sounds the letters out. Only spelling and typing are affected; words are spoken as before. Digits, punctuation and symbols keep their names, and so do the foreign letters *q, w, x, y* (*ku, duplo ve, iks, ipsilon*), which have no sound of their own in the language. A capital letter that the screen reader announces with a word before it ("veliko N" in NVDA, "veliko slovo N" in TalkBack, "veliko početno slovo N" in VoiceOver, "cap N" in English) is spelled like any other letter: by its sound when you chose letter sounds, and at the spelling speed.
- Spelling speed: a new setting in the pause settings sets the rate of spelled and typed characters as a percentage of the speech rate. 100% is the speech rate itself, as before; the default of 50% is a little over half of it, a middle that is neither rushed nor slow.
- Letter names follow the language of the voice. Croatian and Bosnian voices use the names of the Croatian orthography (*a, be, ce, če, će, de, dže, đe, e, ef, ge, ha, i, je, ka, el, elj, em, en, enj, o, pe, er, es, eš, te, u, ve, ze, že*); Serbian voices follow the Board for the Standardization of the Serbian Language (*lje, nje, ša*), for Cyrillic and Latin letters alike. "J" is now *je*, not *jot*. The spelling dictionary still names digits, punctuation and symbols; an entry of yours for a letter still wins, except that an entry which is merely a letter's name gives way when letters are spelled by their sounds.
- The new voices accept a wider range of speech rate and pitch: a quarter to four times the normal value.
- The voice settings are in the same order on every platform: speech rate and acceleration, then pitch and inflection, then volume. The intonation on/off switch of version 1.0 is gone; the inflection level replaces it, and 0% is a monotone.
- Accent dictionary: a new user dictionary, `accents.json`, lets you correct the stress of a word for all its forms at once, or of a whole verb with all its persons and tenses. It is stored next to the other user dictionaries and loaded with them. See section 5.11 of the user guide.
- Numbers are read in the language of the voice: Zvonko says *tisuća* and *milijun*, Stojan and Mirsad *hiljada* and *milion*. One and two now agree with the number word (*dvije tisuće*, *dvadeset jedna tisuća*, *dvije milijarde*).
- A colon or exclamation mark inside a word is always read by name, whatever punctuation level the screen reader uses, as eSpeak does. A period inside a word is read by name only before a file extension or a domain label ("datoteka.txt" is *datoteka točka te iks te*, "pjesma.mp3", "www.index.hr") and as a decimal point; the Serbian and Bosnian voices say *tačka*. Anywhere else a period glued to the next word ends the sentence silently, the way screen readers glue the parts of a label: "Preslušano.Nestajuća poruka" is read as two sentences, without *točka*. A period after a single letter is the dot of an abbreviation: it is silent and the letters are read by name, "s.a.r.s." is *es a er es*, "U.S.A." *u es a*. A period between digits is read by name and every group of digits as a whole number: "3.14" is *tri točka četrnaest*, "1.317" *jedan točka tristo sedamnaest*, a version number or IP address is read group by group; "3,14" is *tri zarez četrnaest*. A comma between the single digits of a phone number, as screen readers send it ("+ 1 2 3,5 6,7 8 9,8 7 6"), is a pause between the groups, not *zarez*. A number with a period before a lowercase word is read as a plain number, with the period silent: "7. listopada 2026." is *sedam listopada dvije tisuće dvadeset šest*. A number glued to letters is set off from them ("u20:03" is *u dvadeset nula tri*, "5kg" *pet ka ge*), and the period that joins a word to such letters, as in a screen reader's label "7.listopada.u20:03", is silent: *sedam listopada u dvadeset nula tri*. Clock times and dates are read without the separators: "12:30" is *dvanaest trideset*, "7.10.2026." and "7. 10. 2026." *sedam deset dvije tisuće dvadeset šest*. The colon of a web address ("https://") is not read. Abbreviations are read as written, not expanded: "dr." is *de er*, "km" *ka em*. Before, every period, comma and colon split the text into clauses, so "datoteka.txt" was spoken as two sentences.
- Fixed: entries added to the user spelling and emoji dictionaries had no effect; they now change how characters and emoji are read.
- Fixed: on the command line and under Speech Dispatcher, Detence, Baba and Đedo sounded exactly like Josip and Vlado.
- Fixed: the newline pause setting had no effect, and a line break was read as a space, so the lines of a post or a list ran together into one sentence. A line break now ends a phrase with that pause, and the next line starts a sentence of its own: in a post whose line "Javno" is followed by the line "S ponosom predstavljam", *S* is the preposition, not the letter *es*.

### Windows (SAPI5 and NVDA)

- The new voices, the singing voices, the Inflection level and Acceleration settings and the accent dictionary are available in SAPI5 applications, in the Laprdus Configurator and in the NVDA add-on. The NVDA add-on picks the new voice of NVDA's language by default and reloads the accent dictionary when the file changes.
- The Laprdus Configurator has the Spelling speed slider in its pause settings and the Spelling choice (letter names or letter sounds) among the options; both apply to SAPI5 and to the NVDA add-on. The command-line tool has the new options `-s` (spell the text), `-S` (spelling speed) and `-m` (spelling mode).
- Fixed: when a program asked SAPI5 to spell only part of a text, Laprdus spelled all of it, and a requested silence was spoken as a space.
- Settings saved by version 1.0 keep their meaning: an unforced speech rate or pitch, which version 1.0 ignored, stays normal instead of changing the rate the screen reader asks for, and intonation switched off becomes an Inflection level of 0%, which the Configurator can raise again. Under NVDA, NVDA's Inflection slider sets the intonation, and NVDA's rate boost, not the Acceleration setting, extends the rate range.

### Linux

- The new voices, the singing voices and the accent dictionary are available in the command-line tool and in Speech Dispatcher. The command-line tool has the new options `-I` (inflection, 0-100), `-a` (acceleration), `-s` (spell the text), `-S` (spelling speed) and `-m` (spelling mode); under Speech Dispatcher the pitch range setting controls the inflection level, and the spelling speed and mode come from `settings.json`.
- Speech Dispatcher now offers Laprdus for Bosnian as well as Croatian and Serbian; the packages add the mapping on upgrade too.

### Android

- The new voices and the singing voices are available as system voices, with the Inflection level and Acceleration settings in the app. The Spelling speed slider is in the reading pauses and the Spelling switch (letter sounds) in the advanced settings.
- Laprdus now offers Bosnian to the system. When an app asks for a language your chosen voice already speaks, that voice is kept instead of being switched.
- Fixed: the spelling and emoji dictionaries edited in the app were not used by the speech service; all three user dictionaries now take effect as soon as a voice is loaded. The accent dictionary is read from the same folder.
- Fixed: the app and the speech service shared one engine, so the Speak button spoke at the rate, pitch and volume of TalkBack's last request, and choosing a voice in the app left TalkBack without your dictionary entries until a voice was loaded again. Each now has an engine of its own: the Speak button speaks with your settings and your dictionaries, and TalkBack switches to the voice you choose with your dictionaries in place.
- A dictionary entry you add, change or delete in the app, and switching the user dictionaries on or off, now take effect in TalkBack at once. Before, they waited until a voice was loaded again.

### iPhone, iPad and Mac

- The new voices and the singing voices appear among the system voices, with the Inflection level and Acceleration settings in the app. The Spelling speed slider is in the reading pauses and the Spelling choice (letter names or letter sounds) in the advanced settings. The accent dictionary is read from the app's dictionary folder.
- Fixed: when VoiceOver read an item that contains a single character to be spelled out, such as a button with a badge count, the whole item was spelled letter by letter, and the name and the type of a control ran together into one phrase. Each part is now read as VoiceOver asks: text as text, the character spelled, pauses as pauses.
- VoiceOver's pitch change for capital letters is now heard, a request that VoiceOver cancels is no longer spoken, and the spelling and emoji dictionaries are loaded together with the pronunciation dictionary.
